1. **Before:** \subsection{Why This Thesis Retains All Eight Emotion Dimensions} \label{subsec:fund_why_not_va} This thesis uses eight emotion dimensions (valence, energy arousal, tension arousal, anger, fear, happiness, sadness, tenderness) rather than only the two of the circumplex model. Three considerations bear on this choice, and not all of them are strictly necessary. \paragraph{Energetic and tense arousal are empirically distinct dimensions.} \citet{schimmack2002experiencing} tested whether energetic and tense arousal can be reduced to valence plus a single arousal factor. Once the variance shared with valence was removed, the remaining correlation between the two was not significantly different from zero. They are two separate dimensions, not one. This is the psychological basis for the three-dimensional model of \citet{eerola2011comparison} (valence, energy arousal, tension arousal). It is also why this thesis keeps tension arousal as a feature of its own instead of folding it into a general ``arousal''. \paragraph{Discrete categories are the more diagnostic description, but not a necessary one.} The clearest evidence comes from this thesis's own analysis (Section~\ref{sec:eval_signatures}). On the dimensional axes, Action and Horror lie close together. Compared with all other clips, both have lower valence (Cohen's $d = -0.42$ and $-0.63$) and higher tension ($d = +0.46$ and $+0.50$). The discrete categories separate the two genres along one axis. Horror is marked by fear ($d = +0.75$, the strongest link between a genre and an emotion in the dataset). In Action, fear is only moderately raised ($d = +0.35$). Anger does not distinguish them at all ($d = +0.34$ against $+0.38$). Fear is thus the most informative single emotion, and the description ``Horror is fear'' says more about the function of a score than ``Horror is low valence and high tension''. This does not mean a classifier needs the discrete categories. Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation} tests this by training the genre classifier on subsets of the eight emotions, and the classifier does not depend on them. Leaving out any single emotion changes the film-level macro-$F_1$ by at most about one hundredth (0.012). The only change that is nominally significant is an increase, when tension is left out. Smaller subsets lose a little, but not significantly: fear alone, or valence and energy alone, keep 96 to 99\,\% of the score of all eight. The eight ratings are strongly correlated with each other (fear rises with tension and falls with valence), so a small subset already contains most of the information relevant to genre. All eight are kept for reasons other than prediction. The discrete categories name the function of a score directly, so they make the model easier to interpret. It cannot be known in advance which subset would suffice. Finally, eleven features add almost no cost to computation or modelling. \paragraph{Hybrid theoretical models support combining both frameworks.} \citet{eerola2011comparison} themselves report that, for averaged listener ratings, their three-dimensional model can be reduced linearly to two dimensions without a significant loss of fit. They also find that the five discrete emotions match regions of this two-dimensional space closely. The ablation result above agrees. The rating space has few dimensions, and a classifier sees the same structure whichever coordinates it is given. The two frameworks do not differ in what they let a model predict. They differ in what they let a person read off the model, and this is the focus of this thesis. This fits hybrid accounts of emotion (discussed in \citealp{eerola2011comparison}). In these accounts, core affect is dimensional, while putting a name to an emotional episode draws on further categorical or appraisal information that the dimensional space does not hold. For these reasons the thesis keeps all eight emotion dimensions that the dataset provides and combines the two frameworks, instead of cutting the features down to valence and arousal. It also reports that this reduction would not have lowered accuracy.
