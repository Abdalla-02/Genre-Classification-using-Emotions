# Comments on `_Thesis_12.11.pdf` (read 14 Nov 2026)

The annotated PDF (Drive, `_Thesis_12.11.pdf`, 109 pages) holds 15 comments by the author,
one of them passing on a rule of the supervisor. Page numbers are those of that PDF. Each
comment is quoted, followed by what was done (Overleaf `332e57b` and `4419a6d`, log section 41).

1. **p. 8, research questions:** "They don't have to be listed separately from the
   explanation, just write them all in normal paragraph form and mark the question numbers."
   The description list and the paragraph after it are now one paragraph per question set in
   prose, with **RQ1** to **RQ4** in bold and each question in italics.
2. **p. 8, RQ3:** "This formulation isn't good, make it more clear and simple." RQ3 now reads:
   how predicting genre from emotions compares with predicting it directly from the same audio
   representation. Still a how-question.
3. **p. 8, "To the author's knowledge":** "Why third person? ... is that a common thing to do
   in theses?" Both are common; the author chose the first person ("To my knowledge"), and
   asked for the first person in the rest of the thesis where it fits (see below).
4. **p. 9, Blockbuster features:** "which audio features? VGGish or MIR? write that down."
   The introduction now names both: VGGish embeddings and hand-crafted MIR descriptors.
5. **p. 11, more visuals in Chapters 2 to 4:** four figures added, all generated and checked
   against the text: Figure 2.2 waveform and log-mel spectrogram of one Eerola clip (Hellraiser,
   Set 1 no. 304; `experiments/make_spectrogram.py`, VGGish front-end settings); Figure 2.3
   five-fold cross-validation grouped by film (TikZ); Figure 2.4 the four outcomes TP/FP/FN/TN
   (TikZ); Figure 4.2 clips per film and films per genre (`make_figures.py data_overview`).
   No figure was taken from another paper.
6. **p. 21, F1:** "explain using TP, TN, FP and FN and also go through precision and recall."
   Done in Fundamentals 2.6.2 with Figure 2.4 and the formulas for precision, recall, F1 and
   macro-F1.
7. **p. 23, formulas:** "Write all formulas in a similar way. Some are written as plain
   text." Every formula is now a numbered display equation, with a "where" list when it has
   more than one symbol: R^2, precision/recall/F1, macro-F1, the random-guess estimate (was
   plain text), ROC-AUC, the all-positive F1, Cohen's d and the corrected t-test; the derived
   features in Methods are numbered too.
8. **p. 24, corrected t-test:** "why is it mentioned? where is it used and what for?"
   Fundamentals now says that the bootstrap is the main test for every comparison and the
   corrected t-test the stricter check beside it, used for the comparisons scored over clips
   because it needs one score per test fold (pointer to Methods 4.6.3).
9. **p. 52, Figure 5.1:** "make the mean separate." The mean is now its own panel.
10. **p. 53, the supervisor's rule for all tables and figures:** numbers the reader can see
    are not repeated in the text. Chapter 5 was gone through figure by figure: levels,
    intervals, precision/recall values and effect sizes that a figure or table shows were
    removed; the headline values (0.460, 0.491, 0.508), differences with their p-values that no
    table shows, and the numbers behind claims were kept.
11. **p. 53, ICC:** "Is that explained in Fundamentals?" Now it is: ICC(C,1) (consistency) and
    ICC(A,1) (absolute agreement) in the R^2 paragraph of 2.6.2.
12. **p. 54, Figure 5.2:** "should be placed one or two paragraphs upwards." It now opens
    Section 5.2 and appears directly under its heading.
13. **p. 60, Figure 5.6:** "we don't need this, only mention the Eerola one, because it's
    valid. Make it better readable and with good quality." The author chose to drop the
    Blockbuster panel. The figure is now one heatmap of all eight Eerola genres from the human
    ratings, vector-drawn with larger numbers and the strongest emotion of each genre boxed; it
    replaces the table of dominant signatures (same values, `results/signatures_eerola.json`).
    The replication stays as numbers in Section 5.5.1.
14. **p. 76, "on par":** "use a synonym." All 12 occurrences replaced ("comparable", "perform
    similarly", "reach similar scores", "close to").
15. **p. 77, calibration:** "I could try to do that in this thesis." The author decided to keep
    it as future work.

**First person (author's request, 14 Nov).** Where the author acts or decides, the thesis now
says "I": the research question ("I address it"), the choice of networks, the hypotheses of the
bottleneck, grouping by film, taking the six shared genres from Ma et al., recovering their rule,
the layer and regressor choices, the sanity check, the later decisions named in the
limitations and in Chapter 7. "This thesis" stays where the document or its contribution is
meant. Every before/after pair of the text is in `docs/review_2026-11-12_pairs.md`.
