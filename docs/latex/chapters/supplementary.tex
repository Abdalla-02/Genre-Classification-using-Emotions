In Table~\ref{tab:blockbuster_reference}, the published values are quoted from
\citet{ma2021computational}, Table~4. \emph{Average pooling} averages the cue vectors of a
film before classifying it, \emph{single attention} learns a weight for each cue, and
\emph{Simple-MI} (simple multiple-instance learning) classifies the set of cues with
$k$-nearest neighbours ($k$NN) or a support vector machine (SVM). The plurality label set
gives every film the most common combination of genres. The direct pipeline lies within the
range of their VGGish models, and both baselines reproduce to within 0.03.

The emotion model is applied per cue, the unit it was trained on. Predicting the emotions of
each cue and averaging them outperforms predicting them once from the film-average embedding
in-domain (0.616 against 0.557, Nadeau--Bengio $p=0.020$). In the zero-shot setting the
difference is small (0.508 against 0.502).

\section{The All-Positive Reference}
\label{sec:app_floor}

A classifier that assigns every genre to every film has a recall of 1 and an $F_1$ of
$2p/(1+p)$ per genre, where $p$ is the share of films with the genre
(Section~\ref{sec:eval_genre}). Table~\ref{tab:app_floor} compares it with the emotion
pipeline for each genre.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \small
  \caption{The all-positive reference and the emotion pipeline per genre.}
  \label{tab:app_floor}
  \begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}
    \toprule
    Genre & Films & All-positive $F_1$ & Emotion $F_1$ & $\Delta$ & $p$ & Emotion AUC \\
    \midrule
    Action & 12 & 0.46 & 0.61 & $+0.147$ & 0.101 & 0.72 \\
    Crime  & 12 & 0.46 & 0.35 & $-0.115$ & 0.103 & 0.47 \\
    Drama  & 29 & 0.84 & 0.76 & $-0.077$ & 0.252 & 0.71 \\
    Comedy & 4  & 0.18 & 0.34 & $+0.146$ & \textbf{0.012} & 0.76 \\
    Horror & 5  & 0.22 & 0.23 & $+0.009$ & 0.916 & 0.61 \\
    \midrule
    Macro  &    & 0.434 & 0.460 & $+0.022$ & 0.518 & 0.655 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

In Table~\ref{tab:app_floor}, $\Delta$ is the emotion pipeline minus the all-positive
classifier, with the $p$-value of the paired film bootstrap. Bold marks $p<0.05$. AUC is the
film-level ROC-AUC, for which the all-positive classifier scores 0.50. The films carry 1.55
genres on average; the emotion pipeline assigns 2.27 per film, the ratings 2.21 and the
direct representations 1.27 to 1.65. On all eight genres the reference reaches 0.338 at film level, against 0.349 for the emotion
pipeline. In film-level macro ROC-AUC the emotion pipeline (0.655) leads direct VGGish by
$+0.071$ ($p=0.093$), MusiCNN by $+0.078$ ($p=0.096$) and the PCA-8 control by $+0.071$
($p=0.087$).

\section{How the Ratings Relate to Each Other}
\label{sec:app_ratings}

Figure~\ref{fig:circumplex} is a schematic. Figure~\ref{fig:rating_structure} shows how the
eight rating scales of the dataset relate to each other over all 360 rated clips, in the plane
of the first two principal components of their correlations (59.8\,\% and 22.6\,\% of the
variance).

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.62\textwidth]{figures/rating_structure.pdf}
  \caption{The eight rating scales in the plane of the first two principal components.}
  \label{fig:rating_structure}
\end{figure}

In Figure~\ref{fig:rating_structure}, each scale is placed by its correlation with the two
components, so a scale close to the circle is described well by this plane. Scales that point
the same way correlate positively, and scales that point in opposite directions negatively.
The arrows are the three dimensional scales, and the grey squares are the five discrete
emotions. The first component runs from tension, fear and anger to valence and tenderness,
the second from sadness to happiness. Unlike in the schematic, valence and tension are almost
opposite ($r=-0.91$), energy correlates with tension ($r=0.58$) rather than with valence
($r=-0.40$), and sadness lies on the pleasant side ($r=0.32$ with valence).
