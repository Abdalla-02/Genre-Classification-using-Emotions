% ---------------------------------------------------------------- METHODS ----
\paragraph{Evaluation protocol and statistical reliability.}
All genre results are obtained under cross-validation grouped by film, so that clips
from the same soundtrack never appear in both the training and the test partition; this
is essential, since an ungrouped split lets a classifier recognise the film rather than
the genre (Section~\ref{subsec:leakage}). A single five-fold run, however, yields only
five score samples, which is too few to distinguish a difference of a few Macro-$F_1$
points from the variation caused by the particular partition. The evaluation is
therefore repeated: the assignment of films to folds is re-randomised ten times, giving
$10\times5=50$ leakage-free folds, and every method is scored on exactly the same folds
so that all comparisons are paired.

Two statistics are reported. Uncertainty on a single method is given as a
$95\,\%$ confidence interval computed from the ten per-repeat means rather than from the
fifty individual folds, because folds within one repeat share almost all of their
training data and are not independent. For a comparison between two methods, the
Nadeau--Bengio corrected resampled $t$-test \citep{nadeau2003inference} is used. The
ordinary paired $t$-test is inadmissible here: it treats the fifty fold-differences as
independent observations although their training sets overlap almost completely, and
consequently reports far too small $p$-values. The correction inflates the variance
estimate by the ratio of test-set to training-set size,
\[
  t \;=\; \frac{\bar{d}}{\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{1}{n}+\dfrac{n_{\text{test}}}{n_{\text{train}}}\right)\sigma^2_{d}}},
\]
where $\bar{d}$ and $\sigma^2_d$ are the mean and variance of the per-fold differences
and $n=50$. Because only the $1/n$ term shrinks when repeats are added, this test has a
lower bound on the attainable $p$-value for a given corpus; that bound is reported as
$p_{\lim}$ and is what makes it possible to distinguish a comparison that needs more
resampling from one that needs more data.

\paragraph{Hyper-parameter selection.}
The inverse regularisation strength $C$ of the logistic regression is not left at
scikit-learn's default but selected per training fold by an inner cross-validation, again
grouped by film -- a nested cross-validation. This matters here: with roughly $330$ clips
against embedding dimensionalities of $128$ to $768$, the default $C=1$ under-regularises
every feature set, and it penalises the high-dimensional embeddings most (tuning raises
the AST baseline by $0.025$ and the CLAP baseline by $0.033$ Macro-$F_1$, against $0.018$
for the eleven emotion features). Selecting $C$ on the test folds would inflate the
reported scores, and leaving it untuned would understate the baselines; the nested
procedure avoids both. The selected value is $C=0.003$ in the large majority of folds.

Four findings follow. First, the ordering of the approaches is \emph{stable}: in every
comparison of an emotion-based approach against a direct-embedding one, the confidence
interval of the difference excludes zero, and the emotion-based pipeline wins between
$80\,\%$ and $88\,\%$ of the individual folds. The advantage of the emotion representation is therefore not an
artefact of one favourable partition, which is what the earlier single five-fold run
could not establish. Second, the three pretrained embeddings are statistically
indistinguishable from one another -- AST $0.345$, VGGish $0.350$, CLAP $0.335$, with
$p=0.752$ between AST and CLAP -- so the comparison is not against one arbitrarily chosen
or poorly suited audio representation: the emotion features outperform all three.
Third, predicted and ground-truth emotions are themselves indistinguishable
($\Delta=-0.003$, $p=0.855$, $54\,\%$ of folds), meaning the emotion values estimated
from audio are as informative for genre as the human ratings -- the central prerequisite
for the two-stage pipeline to be usable without manually annotated emotion data. Fourth,
the comparison against the PCA-8 bottleneck control remains borderline
($\Delta=+0.052$, $p=0.060$, $88\,\%$ of folds): the emotional bottleneck outperforms a
generic low-dimensional one consistently and by a clear margin, but the effect is at the
edge of what this corpus can establish, and it is reported as such rather than as a
significant result.

That remaining uncertainty is a property of the corpus rather than of the evaluation
procedure. Because only the $1/n$ term of the corrected test shrinks with additional
repeats, the attainable $p$-value has a floor for a given dataset; at ten repeats the
test is already within $0.02$ of it. Adding repeats would therefore buy at most a
marginal change and would amount to selecting a conclusion by choosing the number of
resamplings, whereas adding films would not. This agrees with the learning-curve analysis
in Section~\ref{subsec:learning_curve}, where neither model had reached a plateau at the
full corpus size.

On the full eight-genre task the difference is larger and does reach significance:
emotion features attain $0.300$ $[0.293,\,0.306]$ against $0.257$ $[0.251,\,0.263]$ for
the AST embedding, a difference of $+0.043$ that holds in $92\,\%$ of the folds
($p=0.031$). Under the earlier single five-fold protocol the same comparison produced
$p=0.62$, which illustrates how little a five-fold run can say about a difference of this
size.
