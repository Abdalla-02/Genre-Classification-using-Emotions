% ---------------------------------------------------------------- METHODS ----
\paragraph{Evaluation protocol and statistical reliability.}
All genre results are obtained under cross-validation grouped by film, so that clips
from the same soundtrack never appear in both the training and the test partition; this
is essential, since an ungrouped split lets a classifier recognise the film rather than
the genre (Section~\ref{subsec:leakage}). A single five-fold run, however, yields only
five score samples, which is too few to distinguish a difference of a few Macro-$F_1$
points from the variation caused by the particular partition. The evaluation is
therefore repeated: the assignment of films to folds is re-randomised ten times, giving
$10\times5=50$ leakage-free folds, and every method is scored on exactly the same folds
so that all comparisons are paired.

Two statistics are reported. Uncertainty on a single method is given as a
$95\,\%$ confidence interval computed from the ten per-repeat means rather than from the
fifty individual folds, because folds within one repeat share almost all of their
training data and are not independent. For a comparison between two methods, the
Nadeau--Bengio corrected resampled $t$-test \citep{nadeau2003inference} is used. The
ordinary paired $t$-test is inadmissible here: it treats the fifty fold-differences as
independent observations although their training sets overlap almost completely, and
consequently reports far too small $p$-values. The correction inflates the variance
estimate by the ratio of test-set to training-set size,
\[
  t \;=\; \frac{\bar{d}}{\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{1}{n}+\dfrac{n_{\text{test}}}{n_{\text{train}}}\right)\sigma^2_{d}}},
\]
where $\bar{d}$ and $\sigma^2_d$ are the mean and variance of the per-fold differences
and $n=50$. Because only the $1/n$ term shrinks when repeats are added, this test has a
lower bound on the attainable $p$-value for a given corpus; that bound is reported as
$p_{\lim}$ and is what makes it possible to distinguish a comparison that needs more
resampling from one that needs more data.

\subsection{Statistical Reliability of the Comparisons}
\label{subsec:statistical_power}

repeated protocol, and Table~\ref{tab:power_cmp} the corresponding paired comparisons.

Three findings follow. First, the ordering of the approaches is \emph{stable}: for every
comparison in Table~\ref{tab:power_cmp} the confidence interval of the difference
excludes zero, and the emotion-based pipeline wins between $76\,\%$ and $92\,\%$ of the
individual folds. The advantage of the emotion representation is therefore not an
artefact of one favourable partition, which is what the earlier single five-fold run
could not establish. Second, only the comparison against the AST embedding reaches
conventional significance under the corrected test ($\Delta=+0.065$, $p=0.021$); the
comparisons against the PCA-8 bottleneck control ($p=0.158$) and against the direct
VGGish embedding ($p=0.104$) do not. The claim that the emotional bottleneck outperforms
an arbitrary low-dimensional bottleneck is thus supported in direction and magnitude but
not established at the $5\,\%$ level. Third, predicted and ground-truth emotions are
statistically indistinguishable ($\Delta=+0.005$, $p=0.791$, $50\,\%$ of folds): the
emotion values estimated from audio are as informative for genre as the human ratings,
which is the central prerequisite for the two-stage pipeline to be usable without
manually annotated emotion data.

The remaining uncertainty is a property of the corpus, not of the evaluation. Increasing
the number of repeats cannot resolve it: the attainable $p$-values converge to
$p_{\lim}=0.137$ (PCA-8 control) and $p_{\lim}=0.085$ (VGGish direct), so even an
unlimited number of resamplings of these 41 films would leave both comparisons
non-significant. With ten repeats the test is already within $0.02$ of that bound. What
limits the conclusion is therefore the number of distinct films available for grouping,
which agrees with the learning-curve analysis in Section~\ref{subsec:learning_curve},
where neither model had reached a plateau at the full corpus size.

On the full eight-genre task the same pattern holds at a lower level: emotion features
reach $0.276$ $[0.270,\,0.281]$ against $0.240$ $[0.233,\,0.247]$ for the AST embedding,
a difference of $+0.036$ that is directionally consistent ($82\,\%$ of folds) but not
significant ($p=0.092$, $p_{\lim}=0.074$). Under the earlier single five-fold protocol
the same comparison produced $p=0.62$, which illustrates how little a five-fold run can
say about a difference of this size.
